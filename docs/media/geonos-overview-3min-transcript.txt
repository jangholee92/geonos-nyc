Choosing a building means planning for years of heat, heavy rain, and changing needs. But the evidence is scattered across maps, records, and photographs. This is GEONOS. We bring it together to help people make more climate-resilient building decisions.

Here, we’re exploring a cold-storage retrofit in Brooklyn. Muse Voice turns a spoken question into an editable message, while the map keeps both buildings in view.

Across New York, search addresses, filter records, and explore mapped layers in 3D. A second selection opens a same-scale building comparison.

Muse Spark brings official records into the conversation. In this published 2050 rainfall scenario, mapped deep water reaches the ground at 572 Smith’s outline. At 592 Smith, the nearest deep-water class is about forty-seven meters away.

That gives us a useful next step: compare entrance and equipment elevations. A separate footprint requirement helps us see which candidate fits the project’s initial size condition.

Climate Studio combines station history, paired temperature and dew point, and regional future pathways to frame the project’s operating conditions.

Energy reports trace each reporting boundary’s buildings and floor area, guiding requests for meter schedules.

SAM 3.1 marks roof and tree regions in the dated aerials. Compare roof detections with the mapped outlines. Muse Wonder reviews those structured findings alongside building records, helping the team focus its next survey.

DINOv2 compares the 2020 and 2024 images locally in the browser. We can explore the highlighted locations in the original photographs.

Muse Glimmer runs on the local computer. It connects our planning note to building records and owner requests. The team needs twenty thousand square feet of usable space. Here, verifying usable space could change which building we inspect first.

Muse Image turns the written checks into an illustrated inspection plan, making the next steps easier to share with the project team.
