Choosing a building involves heat, heavy rain, and years of changing needs. This is GEONOS. It brings maps, records, and imagery into one workspace, so we can compare two places and decide what to check next.

Select 572 and 592 Smith Street. Their outlines share one scale. The toolbar keeps each analysis in this comparison. Native building IDs keep the selection stable as we move between tools.

Voice opens recording or upload. Muse Voice creates an editable transcript. We review it, then add it to the message. Sending stays a separate action.

Compare asks Muse Spark for official evidence. In the 2050 rainfall scenario, mapped water at least one foot deep touches 572. The matching class is about 47 metres from 592. Next, check entrance and equipment elevations. Sources identify the scenario and the returned records.

Climate connects station observations with regional projections. Energy reports retain their boundaries: the same energy total produces different intensities across two reported floor areas. Units and dates travel with each figure.

Aerial imagery retrieves dated photographs. SAM 3.1 marks roofs and trees. The crosscheck compares returned roof regions with the official outline. Muse Wonder turns the structured findings into survey questions.

Change detection retrieves 2020 and 2024 photographs. Run DINOv2 in the browser, inspect highlighted windows, then move the divider through the source images. The model highlights visual differences in these dated images.

Second review gives Muse Wonder the current public-data brief. Its tradeoff, assumption and decisive check help focus the next inspection.

Inspection visual uses Muse Image to illustrate the written checks. The concept helps discuss drainage, equipment and access with the project team.

Private notes opens the local workflow. Muse Glimmer reviews a twenty-thousand-usable-square-foot requirement. It requests measured plans, clear height and loading dimensions, with a conditional next step based on the usable area.
