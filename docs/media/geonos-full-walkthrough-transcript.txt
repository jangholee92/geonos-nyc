[00:00:00,000 – 00:00:33,125] Climate evidence for building decisions
Heat and flooding shape cold storage decisions. Before choosing a building, bring the records together and decide what to inspect. This is GEONOS. It connects a citywide map, public evidence and six Meta model roles in one investigation. The NYC workspace, evidence pipeline and model integrations were built during the hackathon, using existing public datasets and pretrained models. Let’s follow two Smith Street buildings from their records to a practical site-visit plan.

[00:00:33,125 – 00:01:00,833] Source inventory: 1,082,964 records · Display: 1,082,923 valid outlines
Start with the whole city. More than a million valid building outlines cover all five boroughs. At this scale, density makes the pattern readable. Zoom closer and individual outlines appear. Focus a borough, choose a contextual layer and read its legend. Switch to 3D to explore recorded roof heights.

[00:01:00,833 – 00:01:35,167] Find addresses, building records and tax lots
Search an address, building identification number or tax-lot number. The result identifies which record you’re opening. A tax lot can link to several buildings, so those identifiers help you follow the relationship. Use filters to narrow the records, or Search this view to work with the area on screen. Move through the result pages, select a candidate, and keep your chosen places available as you continue exploring.

[00:01:35,167 – 00:02:05,500] Select one place to inspect and two to compare
Select one building to open its details on the left. Select a second and the panel becomes a comparison, with A and B on the map. Here, we have 572 and 592 Smith Street. Compare their mapped footprints at the same physical scale, then inspect recorded roof height, construction year and zoning. Whole-tax-lot floor area has its own label. Open the source references to follow each measurement back to its record.

[00:02:05,500 – 00:02:40,625] Set conditions and attach dated reports
Choose the measurement that matters to your project. For this demonstration, set a condition of twenty thousand square feet. You can investigate usable space, then check the condition against mapped footprint area. The comparison shows each result and identifies a useful next fact to obtain. Add project requirements, follow their evidence and prepare questions about electrical capacity. An owner report can carry its source and date alongside the public records.

Adjust the investigation band, inspect the scenario assumptions and read the source dates. Use that geographic context to plan entrance, drainage and equipment-elevation checks.

[00:03:19,542 – 00:03:51,250] Connect observed station history and regional projections
Climate history adds the station perspective. Explore NOAA observations by year, month and metric, with period labels beside the charts. Then move to the regional future view, where published NPCC projections show their own time windows and baseline. Together, these views help you frame the operating horizon: what indoor conditions will the project need, which equipment questions deserve attention, and what should the team investigate for the period ahead?

The chart also provides source coverage and quality information. Bring these paired observations into conversations about operating conditions, equipment performance and the measurements to collect at the building.

[00:04:49,208 – 00:05:17,708] Muse Voice · Audio to an editable question

Muse Spark carries the conversation into the investigation, choosing tools for official records and evidence. Expand Sources to inspect the retrieved information and follow the tool trace. The chat stays alongside the map, so you can keep the selected buildings in view while refining the next question.

These views give the project team specific places to examine during a roof, vegetation and equipment survey.

[00:06:22,375 – 00:07:08,250] DINOv2 · Compare dated visual features in your browser

Then prepare a site-visit question based on the areas you want to examine more closely.
